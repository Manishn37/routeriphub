How to Use the Public IP Address & Geolocation Checker

Every device connected to the Internet requires an Internet Protocol (IP) address to route data packets between servers, websites, and client computers. This free utility lets you instantly inspect any public IPv4 or IPv6 address or analyze your own active internet connection without running third-party tracking scripts:

  1. Detect Your Own IP: Click "My Public IP" to automatically discover the public WAN IP assigned to your cable modem or fiber gateway by your Internet Service Provider (ISP).
  2. Inspect Any Remote IP or Hostname: Type any IPv4 address (e.g. 8.8.8.8), IPv6 address, or domain name (e.g. github.com) into the search field and click "Inspect IP".
  3. Analyze Routing & Geolocation Data: The tool queries authoritative BGP and Whois records to display your Autonomous System Number (ASN), ISP name, approximate city/region coordinates, and local timezone.
  4. Identify Private Local Subnets: If you enter a private LAN address (such as 192.168.1.1 or 10.0.0.1), the tool immediately classifies the RFC 1918 subnet and provides local router configuration instructions.

Public WAN IP vs. Private Default Gateway (192.168.1.1)

A common source of confusion in computer networking is the difference between your Public IP and your Private Gateway IP:

🌐 Public IP (WAN)

  • Assigned globally by your ISP (Comcast, AT&T, Verizon, Spectrum).
  • Globally unique and routable across the worldwide Internet.
  • Shared by all devices connected to your home Wi-Fi network via Network Address Translation (NAT).
  • Determines your external geographic location visible to websites and streaming services.

🏠 Private IP / Gateway (LAN)

  • Assigned locally by your router's internal DHCP server (e.g. 192.168.1.1).
  • Only accessible within your local home or office physical network.
  • Non-routable over the public Internet (governed by RFC 1918 specifications).
  • Used to open your router's administrative setup dashboard and change Wi-Fi settings.

Understanding Autonomous System Numbers (ASN) & BGP Routing

The global Internet is composed of thousands of independently operated networks called Autonomous Systems (AS). Each Autonomous System is assigned a globally unique 16-bit or 32-bit Autonomous System Number (ASN) by regional internet registries (such as ARIN, RIPE NCC, and APNIC).

Using the Border Gateway Protocol (BGP), these autonomous systems advertise IP prefix blocks to one another. When you perform an IP lookup on this tool, the ASN identifies the primary network operator (such as AS7922 for Comcast, AS7018 for AT&T, AS15169 for Google, or AS13335 for Cloudflare) responsible for routing packets to and from that IP address.

Frequently Asked Questions About Public IP Addresses

Can someone find my exact home street address from my IP?

No. Public IP geolocation databases map IP addresses to the ISP's local point of presence (PoP), central routing exchange, or metropolitan city center. Geolocation is generally accurate to the city or postal area level, but cannot reveal your physical street address or house number without a court subpoena served directly to your ISP.

Why is my IP address different when connected to a VPN?

When using a Virtual Private Network (VPN), your computer establishes an encrypted tunnel to a remote server. All your outbound internet traffic exits through that VPN server, so websites and lookup tools see the VPN provider's IP address and location instead of your real ISP-assigned IP.

How can I change my public IP address?

Most residential internet plans use dynamic IP addressing. You can often obtain a new public IP by powering off your cable/fiber modem for 15–30 minutes (allowing the ISP DHCP lease to expire) before turning it back on, or by cloning a different MAC address into your router's WAN settings.
