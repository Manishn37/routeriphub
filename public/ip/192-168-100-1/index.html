192.168.100.1 – Router Admin Login Page

Home › IP Directory › 192.168.100.1
Written & Tested by Vikas Behl, 12+ Yrs Exp, Senior Technical Support & Network Engineer Reviewed by RouterIPHub Technical Lab • Last Updated November 2026
192.168.100.1 is usually not your router's login page. On most cable setups, 192.168.100.1 is the built-in status page of the cable modem. It shows signal levels, logs and connection status, and it often has no Wi-Fi settings at all. A few fiber ONTs and ISP gateways, notably Huawei units, also use 192.168.100.1, and those do have a real login.

Quick Answer: Open 192.168.100.1

  1. Connect to the modem or gateway by Ethernet if possible, or by its Wi-Fi. Turn off mobile data and any VPN.
  2. Type 192.168.100.1 into the address bar, not the search bar.
  3. Read what loads. A status page means you reached a cable modem. A login page means you reached a gateway or ONT.
  4. If it fails, plug your computer directly into the modem.
Fastest diagnostic step: Plug a laptop into the modem with Ethernet, open http://192.168.100.1, and read the downstream and upstream signal levels before you call your ISP.

What Is 192.168.100.1?

192.168.100.1 is a private IPv4 address in the 192.168.x.x range. Cable modems (DOCSIS 3.0 and 3.1) use 192.168.100.1 as a fixed management address. It works from your local network even when the broadband connection is down.

Think of 192.168.100.1 as a diagnostic window on the modem. It tells you whether the coax line is healthy. Your router at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 controls Wi-Fi and DHCP.

In most setups, 192.168.100.1 stays reachable through a router, but some routers and ISP gateways block it. If 192.168.100.1 fails, try a direct Ethernet connection.

What 192.168.100.1 shows What it means Next step

Find Your Real Gateway Before Using 192.168.100.1

Your default gateway is usually your wireless router, not 192.168.100.1. Check it first using your operating system:

  • Windows: Open Command Prompt, run ipconfig, and read Default Gateway.
  • Mac: Go to System Settings > Wi-Fi > Details > TCP/IP and read Router.
  • iPhone / iPad: Go to Settings > Wi-Fi, tap the (i) icon, and read Router.
  • Android: Go to Wi-Fi, tap the network gear icon, open Advanced, and read Gateway.

If the gateway is 192.168.1.1, 192.168.0.1 or 10.0.0.1, use that address for Wi-Fi, passwords and port forwarding. Use 192.168.100.1 only for the modem. You can also run our automated Detect Gateway Tool.

How to Log In to 192.168.100.1 on PC and Mac

You get the most reliable 192.168.100.1 session from a computer wired straight to the modem. Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all work.

Windows PC

  1. Plug an Ethernet cable from your PC into the modem's LAN port.
  2. Open a browser and type http://192.168.100.1 in the address bar.
  3. Press Enter and wait for the page.
  4. Sign in if 192.168.100.1 shows a login box.
  5. If 192.168.100.1 times out, run ipconfig and confirm the gateway matches 192.168.100.1.

Mac

  1. Connect your Mac to the modem by Ethernet. Use a USB-C adapter if needed.
  2. Open Safari or Chrome and enter http://192.168.100.1.
  3. Sign in if prompted.
  4. If 192.168.100.1 will not open, check System Settings > Network > Details > TCP/IP for the router address.
Static IP trick for 192.168.100.1: If a direct wired connection still fails, set your computer's IP manually to 192.168.100.10 with netmask 255.255.255.0. Then reload 192.168.100.1. Switch back to automatic (DHCP) when you finish.

How to Log In to 192.168.100.1 on Mobile (Android & iPhone)

A phone can reach 192.168.100.1 only when it sits on the modem's own network. A phone on your wireless router's Wi-Fi often cannot open 192.168.100.1 on a modem-only device.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open Settings > Wi-Fi and join the gateway's network.
  2. Turn off Low Data Mode and any active VPN.
  3. Open Safari and enter 192.168.100.1.
  4. Sign in if prompted.

Android

  1. Open Settings > Wi-Fi and join the gateway's network.
  2. Turn off mobile data so the phone stays on the local LAN.
  3. Open Chrome and enter http://192.168.100.1.
  4. Sign in if prompted.
Direct connection note: On a modem-only device such as an Arris SB8200 or Netgear CM1000, phones on your router's Wi-Fi usually cannot reach 192.168.100.1 because the router separates the subnets. Use a laptop on Ethernet for 192.168.100.1.

192.168.100.1 Default Credentials

Check the label on the device first. These are common manufacturer defaults for 192.168.100.1, not guarantees:

Device at 192.168.100.1 Username Password
Arris SURFboard (S33, S34, SB8200) admin password, or the last eight digits of serial number
Motorola cable modems admin password, motorola or blank
Netgear cable modems admin password (CM2000 uses unique label password)
TP-Link cable modems admin admin or password
Huawei ONTs and gateways Varies, often set by ISP Varies, often printed on device label

Many status pages need no login. Some ISPs lock gateway credentials, so call your provider if the label does not work. Newer Arris models require a unique password after first sign-in.

What You Can Check at 192.168.100.1

Cable modem status pages at 192.168.100.1 show live DOCSIS signal data:

192.168.100.1 metric What it tells you
Downstream power (dBmV) Signal strength from the street. A healthy range is typically −7 to +7 dBmV.
Signal-to-noise ratio (dB) Signal quality. Values above 33 dB, ideally 36 dB or more, support stable speeds.
Upstream power (dBmV) Strength of your transmit signal. A healthy range is 40 to 50 dBmV. Above 51 dBmV causes drops.
Locked channels Whether downstream and upstream channels are bonded and synchronized.
Event log T3 and T4 timeouts, DHCP errors, SYNC loss and automatic reboots.
Software version Firmware provisioned directly by your ISP.

Reading these numbers helps when you call support. Healthy ranges vary by provider, so ask your ISP for its targets before booking a technician.

Change Wi-Fi Settings at 192.168.100.1

This applies only if 192.168.100.1 is a combined gateway or fiber ONT with a login:

  1. Sign in at http://192.168.100.1 with the admin credentials.
  2. Open Wi-Fi, Wireless or WLAN.
  3. Pick the 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz band and edit the SSID and passphrase.
  4. Choose WPA2-PSK (AES) or WPA3-Personal and click Save.
  5. Reconnect your devices with the new password.

If 192.168.100.1 is a standalone modem with no Wi-Fi, log in to your separate router at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 instead.

Basic Setup Steps Before Opening 192.168.100.1

  1. Connect the coaxial cable (or fiber) firmly to the modem or ONT.
  2. Power on the modem and wait several minutes for the Power, Downstream, Upstream and Online lights to turn solid.
  3. Connect your router's WAN port to the modem's LAN port with Ethernet.
  4. Power on the router and finish setup at its own address, usually 192.168.1.1.
  5. Open http://192.168.100.1 to inspect signal levels if the connection drops.
  6. Call your ISP to activate the modem's MAC address if the Online light keeps blinking.

Troubleshooting 192.168.100.1

1. 192.168.100.1 will not load

Cause: A separate router drops the request, you are on guest Wi-Fi, or you are on mobile data.

Fix: Connect your laptop to the modem's Ethernet port, turn off any VPN and disable cellular data. If 192.168.100.1 still fails, power-cycle the modem for 30 seconds. The modem ties its 192.168.100.1 page to your router's MAC address, so a new router or firmware often breaks access until a restart.

2. 192.168.100.1 loads but shows no settings

Cause: Standalone modems give read-only data.

Fix: Manage Wi-Fi, port forwarding and DHCP from your router's own address, such as 192.168.1.1.

3. Poor signal values at 192.168.100.1

Cause: A loose coax connector, too many splitters, or line damage near the street tap.

Fix: Tighten the connectors, remove unneeded splitters, and call your ISP with the 192.168.100.1 downstream dBmV and SNR readings.

4. Modem keeps restarting

Cause: A weak power supply, overheating, or upstream power surges.

Fix: Give the modem airflow, plug it straight into a wall outlet, check the event log, and ask your ISP for a line check.

5. Login rejected at 192.168.100.1

Cause: The ISP changed the remote-management credentials.

Fix: Check the label password, or ask support for customer admin credentials.

Factory Reset for 192.168.100.1 Gateways

Use a reset only when you cannot recover access to a combined gateway:

  1. Keep the device powered on.
  2. Find the recessed Reset pinhole on the back.
  3. Hold it for 10 to 15 seconds with a straightened paperclip.
  4. Wait several minutes while the device restarts and re-locks channels.
  5. Sign in with the label credentials and set up again.
Warning: A reset can erase Wi-Fi settings and, on some fiber ONTs, ISP provisioning files. Do not reset a modem just to read signal levels, because the 192.168.100.1 status page rarely needs a login.

Common 192.168.100.1 Misspellings

If you get search results instead of the page, check for these mistakes:

  • 192.168.100.1.1 is an invalid five-octet address.
  • 192.168.l00.1 uses a lowercase L instead of the digit 1.
  • 192.168.10.1 is a different private gateway subnet.
  • 192.168.1.100 is a typical DHCP host address, not 192.168.100.1.
  • 192.168.100.01 has a leading zero that modern web browsers reject.
  • 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 is your wireless router, not the modem at 192.168.100.1.

Frequently Asked Questions About 192.168.100.1

What is 192.168.100.1?

192.168.100.1 is a private IPv4 address that most cable modems use for a local status page. Some ONTs and gateways use 192.168.100.1 for a full login.

Is 192.168.100.1 my router login?

Usually not. Your router normally sits at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1, and 192.168.100.1 belongs to the modem.

What is the default password for 192.168.100.1?

Most devices use admin with password, but the label wins. Arris S33 and S34 units may use the last eight serial digits.

Can I open 192.168.100.1 from outside my home?

No. 192.168.100.1 is private, so keep admin pages off the public internet.

Why does 192.168.100.1 work only on Ethernet?

Some devices and routers block 192.168.100.1 over Wi-Fi, or your router sits between you and the modem.

How do I open 192.168.100.1 on my phone?

Join the modem's own Wi-Fi, switch off mobile data, and enter 192.168.100.1 in Chrome or Safari.

Why can't I reach 192.168.100.1 after changing routers?

The modem often locks 192.168.100.1 to the old router's MAC address. Power-cycle the modem for 30 seconds.

Is 192.168.100.1 safe?

Yes, from inside your own network. Change default passwords on any device that has a login.

Bottom Line on 192.168.100.1

192.168.100.1 is the local address of many cable modems and some ISP gateways. Use 192.168.100.1 to check signal and connection health, not to manage home Wi-Fi. If 192.168.100.1 loads a status page, use your router's own address for settings.
