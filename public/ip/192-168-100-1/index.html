192.168.100.1: Router Admin Login, Default Credentials & Setup Guide

VB
Written & Tested by Vikas Behl 12+ Yrs Exp
Senior Technical Support & Network Engineer
Reviewed by RouterIPHub Technical Lab ✓ Hardware Verified
Last Updated November 2026
192.168.100.1 is usually not your router's login page. On most cable setups it is the built-in status page of the cable modem itself. It shows signal levels, logs, and connection status, and it often has no Wi-Fi settings at all. A few fiber ONTs and ISP gateways, notably Huawei units, also use this address, and those do have a real login.

Quick Answer

  1. Connect to the modem or gateway by Ethernet if possible, or by its Wi-Fi. Turn off mobile data and any VPN.
  2. Type 192.168.100.1 into the address bar, not the search bar.
  3. Read what loads. A status page means you reached a cable modem. A login page means you reached a gateway or ONT.
  4. If it fails, check that your computer is connected directly to the modem or gateway.
Fastest diagnostic step: Plug a laptop directly into the modem with Ethernet, open http://192.168.100.1, and read the downstream and upstream signal levels before calling your ISP.

What Is 192.168.100.1?

It is a private IPv4 address in the 192.168.x.x range. Cable modems (DOCSIS 3.0 and DOCSIS 3.1) use it as an industry-standard fixed management address, and it works from your local network even when the broadband internet connection is completely down.

Think of it as a diagnostic window on your modem. It tells you whether the coaxial line is healthy, while your router at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 controls your local Wi-Fi and DHCP client devices.

It reaches the modem even when you are behind a separate router in most setups through WAN-to-LAN bridging, but some routers and ISP gateways block it. If it fails to load, try a direct wired Ethernet connection.

A login prompt for a gateway A combined modem and router, or an ONT Sign in to manage Wi-Fi and network security
Nothing loads Wrong network or blocked by router firewall Connect directly to the modem by Ethernet

Find Your Real Gateway

Your default gateway is usually your wireless router, not 192.168.100.1. Verify your network gateway address to configure Wi-Fi and passwords:

  • Windows: Open Command Prompt, run ipconfig, and read Default Gateway.
  • Mac: Go to System Settings > Wi-Fi > Details > TCP/IP and read Router.
  • iPhone / iPad: Go to Settings > Wi-Fi, tap the (i) icon next to your network, and read Router.
  • Android: Go to Wi-Fi > tap network gear icon > Advanced and read Gateway.

If the gateway is 192.168.1.1, 192.168.0.1, or 10.0.0.1, use that address to manage your Wi-Fi name, password, and port forwarding.

How to Log In

Windows or Mac

  1. Connect by Ethernet directly to the modem or gateway if you can.
  2. Open any browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox).
  3. Enter http://192.168.100.1 into the address bar.
  4. Sign in if a login page appears.

iPhone & iPad

  1. Open Settings > Wi-Fi and join the gateway's network.
  2. Open Safari and enter 192.168.100.1.
  3. Sign in if prompted.

Android

  1. Open Settings > Wi-Fi and join the gateway's network.
  2. Open Chrome and enter 192.168.100.1.
  3. Sign in if prompted.
Direct Connection Note: On a modem-only device (like an Arris SB8200 or Netgear CM1000), phones on your wireless router's Wi-Fi often cannot reach 192.168.100.1 due to router NAT separation. Use a laptop connected directly via Ethernet to the modem's LAN port.

Default Credentials

Check the label on the device first. These are common manufacturer defaults, not guarantees:

Device Username Password
Arris and Motorola cable modems admin (if prompted) password
Netgear and TP-Link cable modems admin (if prompted) password or admin
Huawei ONTs and gateways Varies, often set by ISP Varies, often printed on the label
ISP broadband gateways admin Printed on device sticker
Many modem status pages None None (read-only diagnostic access)

Many modem status pages need no login at all. Some ISPs lock gateway credentials, so ask your broadband provider if the printed label does not grant entry.

What You Can Check

Cable modem status pages provide real-time DOCSIS RF signal metrics:

Item What it tells you
Downstream power (dBmV) Signal strength coming in from the street; healthy range is typically −7 to +7 dBmV. Very high or low values suggest line problems.
Signal-to-noise ratio (dB) Signal quality over background noise; values above 33 dB (ideally 36+ dB) are required for stable speeds. Low values suggest noise.
Upstream power (dBmV) Strength of the signal you transmit back to the ISP CMTS; healthy range is 40 to 50 dBmV. Above 51 dBmV causes drops.
Locked channels Indicates whether DOCSIS downstream and upstream channels are successfully bonded and synchronized.
Event log Records critical T3 and T4 timeouts, DHCP errors, SYNC loss events, and automatic modem reboots.
Software version Current firmware version pushed and provisioned by your Internet Service Provider.

Reading these numbers helps when you call your ISP technical support. Exact healthy ranges vary by provider, so ask them for their specific target tolerances before scheduling a technician.

Change Wi-Fi Settings

If 192.168.100.1 is a combined gateway or fiber ONT with a login interface:

  1. Sign in at http://192.168.100.1 using administrator credentials.
  2. Open Wi-Fi, Wireless, or WLAN settings.
  3. Select the 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz frequency band and edit the SSID network name and passphrase.
  4. Choose WPA2-PSK (AES) or WPA3-Personal encryption and click Save.
  5. Reconnect your devices using the updated credentials.

If your device is only a standalone modem (no built-in Wi-Fi), log in to your separate router instead (usually at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1).

Basic Setup Steps

  1. Connect the incoming coaxial cable (or optical fiber) firmly to the modem or ONT input port.
  2. Power on the modem and wait several minutes for the Power, Downstream, Upstream, and Online lights to turn solid.
  3. Connect your wireless router's WAN/Internet port to the modem's LAN port with an Ethernet cable.
  4. Power on the router and complete setup at its own admin address (192.168.1.1).
  5. Open http://192.168.100.1 in your browser to inspect signal levels if the internet connection drops or stutters.
  6. Contact your ISP to activate and provision the modem MAC address if the Online light continues to blink.

Troubleshooting

1. 192.168.100.1 will not load

Cause: You are behind a separate router that drops WAN management packets, connected to guest Wi-Fi, or on mobile data.

Fix: Connect your laptop directly to the modem's Ethernet LAN port, disconnect any active VPN, and disable cellular data.

2. Page loads but has no settings

Cause: Standalone modems provide read-only diagnostic telemetry without configurable routing or Wi-Fi features.

Fix: Manage Wi-Fi, port forwarding, and DHCP settings from your router's own gateway IP address (192.168.1.1).

3. Poor signal values (T3/T4 timeouts)

Cause: A loose or damaged coaxial connector, excessive signal splitters, or external line degradation from the street tap.

Fix: Tighten coax connectors with a wrench, remove unneeded RF splitters, and call your broadband ISP with downstream dBmV and SNR logs.

4. Modem keeps restarting

Cause: Flaky power supply, device overheating, or severe upstream power surges triggering modem safety reboots.

Fix: Ensure adequate ventilation, plug directly into a wall outlet rather than a overloaded strip, inspect event logs, and request an ISP line audit.

5. Login rejected on gateway / ONT

Cause: The broadband provider customized default TR-069 management credentials via remote firmware push.

Fix: Verify credentials printed on the device label, or call customer support to request customer-facing admin credentials.

Factory Reset

Use this hardware recovery method only when you cannot recover access to a combined gateway:

  1. Keep the modem powered on.
  2. Locate the recessed Reset pinhole on the rear panel.
  3. Hold the button for 10 to 15 seconds with a straightened paperclip.
  4. Wait several minutes for the modem to restart, cycle LEDs, and re-lock DOCSIS channels.
  5. Log in with the default credentials on the label and reconfigure your settings.
Warning: A reset can erase custom Wi-Fi settings and, on some fiber ONTs, ISP provisioning files, requiring a call to your provider to reactivate. Do not reset a modem just to read signal levels, because the status page does not require a login.

Common Misspellings

If you get search results instead of the diagnostic page, check for these syntax mistakes:

  • 192.168.100.1.1 — An invalid five-octet IP address.
  • 192.168.l00.1 — Typing a lowercase l instead of the digit 1.
  • 192.168.10.1 — A different Class C private gateway (192.168.10.1 guide).
  • 192.168.1.100 — A typical host IP assigned by DHCP to a computer or printer.
  • 192.168.100.01 — Includes an unneeded leading zero rejected by web browsers.
  • 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 — Your wireless router, not your cable modem.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 192.168.100.1?

It is a private IPv4 address that most cable modems use for a local status page. Some ONTs and gateways use it for a full login.

Is it my router login?

Usually not. Your router is normally at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1.

Can I open it from outside my home?

No. It is private. Avoid exposing admin pages to the internet.

Why does it work only on Ethernet?

Some devices and routers block it over Wi-Fi, or your router sits between you and the modem.

Is it safe?

Yes, from inside your own network. Keep default passwords changed on any device that has a login.

Bottom Line

192.168.100.1 is the local address of many cable modems and some ISP gateways. It is best for checking signal and connection health, not for managing your home Wi-Fi. If it loads a status page, use your router's own address for settings.

Fastest diagnostic step: Plug a laptop directly into the modem with Ethernet, open http://192.168.100.1, and read the downstream and upstream signal levels before calling your ISP.
